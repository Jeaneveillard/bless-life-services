Notary Public

Commissioned in Massachusetts. Acknowledgments, jurats, signature witnessing, oaths and affirmations — at our location or yours.

What we can and cannot do

A notary verifies who signed a document. A notary is not an attorney and may not draft your document, choose which form you need, or explain what it means. If you need that, you need a lawyer — and we will say so rather than guess.

What to bring

  • Government-issued photo ID that is current and not expired.
  • The complete document — every page, nothing filled in afterwards.
  • An unsigned signature line. You sign in front of the notary, not before.
  • Any additional signers, present at the same appointment.

Common questions

What do I need to bring to a notary appointment?

Bring a valid, unexpired government-issued photo ID (driver's license, state ID, or passport) and the complete, unsigned document. Do not sign before you arrive — the notary must witness the signature. If the document requires witnesses, plan to bring them or let us know in advance.

Do you travel for notary appointments?

Yes. We provide mobile notary service throughout Massachusetts — homes, hospitals, nursing facilities, offices and other locations. A travel fee applies based on distance; we quote it upfront before you book.
